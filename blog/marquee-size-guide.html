What size marquee do you actually need?

By Sim, White Canvas Events UK  ·  Published 11 September 2026  ·  6 min read

If you've searched "what size marquee do I need" you've probably found answers like "small, medium or large" with no actual numbers attached. We don't think that's very useful either, so here's exactly how many guests each of our real marquee sizes seats and stands — the same sizes we hire out across Derby & the Midlands, with nothing rounded up to make the numbers look better.

The short answer: our smallest 4m x 6m marquee seats 24 guests (35 standing), and our sizes scale up through the range to a 6m x 14m marquee seating 100 (140 standing). For 150 to 300+ guests we move into bespoke large marquees, planned and priced individually because every event that size is different. Seated vs standing: which number should you use?

The two figures aren't interchangeable, so it's worth knowing which one applies to your event:

  • Seated capacity assumes round tables — typically 8 to 12 guests per table, the same as our own hire furniture (a 6ft round seats 10–12, a 5ft round seats 8–10) — with a working aisle left for catering staff. This is the figure to use for a wedding breakfast, sit-down dinner or formal seated event.
  • Standing capacity assumes a drinks-reception style layout, with space to actually move around and mingle rather than being packed in shoulder to shoulder. This is the figure to use for an evening reception, cocktail-style party or networking event with no set seating.

If your event has both — a seated meal followed by an evening party — size for the seated number first, since that's the more space-hungry layout of the two.

What else affects how many people actually fit

The figures above assume just tables and chairs, or just guests standing — nothing extra. A few things will eat into that capacity, so it's worth planning around them rather than being caught out on the day:

  • A dance floor, DJ booth or live band. All of these take up real floor space, so if your evening do includes one, we'd generally recommend sizing up a tier from the guest-count table rather than cutting it fine.
  • A bar or buffet station. Catering areas need their own footprint plus queuing space in front of them.
  • Extra furniture. A gift table, cake table, cloakroom rail or photo booth all take a bite out of your usable floor space.
  • Accessibility needs. Wider aisles for wheelchair access or pushchairs reduce how tightly tables can be packed in.

None of this is a reason to avoid the extras you actually want — it's just easier to plan for them from the start than to discover the numbers don't quite work once everything is set up.
